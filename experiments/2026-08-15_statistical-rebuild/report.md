## 3. Permutation test (replaces the independence baseline)

Swapping the two judge verdicts within each case is exchangeable under the null that the reference shown does not matter.

- Observed PFA: **16.0%**
- Permutation null: mean 17.4%, 95% range [14.3%, 20.4%] over 10000 permutations
- p(null >= observed) = 0.8328; p(null <= observed) = 0.2138
- For comparison, the report's analytic independence baseline (41.2% x (1 - 44.0%)) = **23.1%**

**This corrects the experiment write-up's section 4.3(c).** That section compares the observed 16.0% against an analytic baseline of 23.1% built by multiplying the two marginal flag rates, concludes the detector performs *below chance*, and infers from the shortfall that the two judge calls must be positively correlated.

The multiplication is only valid if the two calls are independent - the very thing the argument goes on to deny. The permutation null does not need that assumption: it swaps both verdicts together, so whatever correlation exists between the two calls is preserved inside the null. Its mean is 17.4%, not 23.1%.

Against that correct null, the observed 16.0% sits **inside** the 95% range [14.3%, 20.4%] (p = 0.214). The detector is therefore **not** below chance. The defensible claim is stronger and simpler: *paired flag accuracy is statistically indistinguishable from a null in which the reference shown to the judge has no effect on its verdict.* The paper should make that claim and drop the below-chance framing, which a reviewer would reject.

## 4. How far the score duplicates the category

- Score histogram, all judge calls: {1: 41, 2: 249, 3: 117, 4: 248, 5: 68, 6: 2, 7: 1, 8: 2, 9: 16, 10: 38}
- Score histogram, scored cases only: {2: 247, 3: 112, 4: 238, 5: 62, 6: 1, 7: 1, 8: 2, 9: 16, 10: 35} (**9 of 10** values ever used; 92.3% of the mass sits in 2-5)
- H(score) = 2.374 bits; I(score; category) = 1.374 bits
- The category explains **57.9%** of the score's entropy

The rubric binds score ranges to categories (`.claude/agents/vuln-judge.md` steps 2-4), and that binding is visible: the category fixes most of the score, and on the scored subset the scale collapses onto a handful of values with a gap where 6-8 should be. ROC-AUC is therefore **substantially redundant with** Paired Flag Accuracy rather than independent evidence for it - the write-up's section 4.3(a)/(b)/(c) are better described as one demonstration examined three ways than as three independent ones. It is not a *complete* relabelling (42.1% of the score's entropy is unexplained by category), so the AUC is not worthless - just not corroborating. WP5-R1 decouples the two so it can be.
